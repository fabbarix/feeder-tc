Responsive system · phone · tablet · desktop

Feeder at every width, not just two

design/mock-screens.html approved phone and desktop. The owner then opened the live app on an iPad at ~1378px and found it neither — two-column recipe cards with a clipped STAPLE tag, three stacked full-width blocks above the Ingredients list, a Location filter that can't fit its own fourth option, and a seven-day week squeezed to ~90px a day. Every one of those is the same root cause: the app has one breakpoint (768px) and treats a tablet as a narrow desktop. This file adds the missing middle tier and shows all ten screens through it. It extends the approved mock — same tokens, same components — it does not replace it.

The three tiers

Breakpoints picked from what actually stops fitting, not from device names — an iPad is not a small desktop and a phone-plus is not a small tablet.

Breakpoints
Phone · 0–767px (unchanged)

One-handed thumb reach is the primary input model here — UI_DESIGN.md §1's mobile-first rule and §5's in-store shopping case both assume it. Bottom tab bar, FABs and bottom sheets are all reach-optimised; a pointer-oriented top nav has nowhere to put six links plus a workbook switcher below this width without wrapping. Nothing about this tier changes — the phone frames below are copied unchanged from the approved mock.

Tablet · 768–1439px (new)

Wide enough for a persistent top nav — brand, six links and a workbook switcher need roughly 650px+ to lay out on one line without wrapping — but not wide enough to show seven day columns, a 250–300px context rail, and a multi-column card grid all at comfortable widths simultaneously. This is exactly the band the app currently skips: every one of its 12 min-width:768px rules keeps firing, unchanged, all the way to whatever comes next, so a tablet silently inherits the desktop layout. The owner's 1378px iPad sits in the middle of this tier — comfortably clear of both edges, so the fixes below aren't tuned to one specific device width.

Desktop · 1440px+ (was ~1680px-tuned, now scales down to 1440)

The point past which a full seven-column week (7 × ~170px comfortable + 6 gaps) fits beside a capped 250–300px rail without any column dropping below a legible width. It's deliberately not 1280 — a 12.9" iPad Pro in landscape is 1366px, and a large tablet is still a touch-primary device held or propped, not a desktop with a mouse and full peripheral vision. The line is about input model and layout headroom, not raw pixels, and 1440 is comfortably past every current tablet's landscape width.

What's actually broken, precisely. Four things, all traced in the live CSS, not guessed: (1) AppShell.module.css's .mainMeasure caps content at 840px from 768px up — correct for prose, wrong for Ingredients, which inherits it because it isn't in AppShell.tsx's WIDE_ROUTES, so its list sits in an 840px column with ~270px of dead margin on either side at 1378px. (2) recipes.module.css's .tagRow sets flex-wrap:nowrap;overflow:hidden — correct on .cardMeta right above it (the "one line, never two" rule), copied by mistake onto the tag pills, which the approved mock deliberately wraps (.rtags{flex-wrap:wrap}). That's the literal "STAPLE" → "STAPLI" clip. (3) pantry.module.css's .layout splits minmax(0,2fr) minmax(220px,1fr) — a proportional third of the viewport for the rail, so it grows without bound as the viewport does, instead of a capped width. (4) SegmentedControl.module.css's .group is inline-flex with no wrap and no per-segment flex-grow — a fourth option in a narrow rail has nowhere to go but overflow or crowd. None of these four need a new component; they need the tablet-tier rules below.

Storage and loading, per DESIGN_PRODUCTS.md §5 — reused, not reinvented. A photo is a 512px WebP capped to a 32KB byte budget (encoder steps quality 85→35 at 512px, then downscales to 448 → 384 → 320px if it still doesn't fit, same ladder already settled for product photos), because a Google Sheets cell holds 50,000 characters and base64 inflation puts the real ceiling at ~36.6KB — a hard limit, not a preference, so there's no case for a bigger budget just because this hero renders larger on the detail page than a product thumbnail does. Photos live in their own RecipePhotos sheet, same reasoning as ProductPhotos: readAll() pulls a whole sheet, and a photo column on the Recipes sheet itself would drag all 18 images down the wire just to list recipes. The grid never fetches a photo it isn't showing. Recipes render immediately from the text-only list; a card whose recipe has a photo shows Skeleton in the media box until that one photo is fetched — lazily, only once the card is on screen — then crossfades in (opacity, 220ms, the kit's own State-tier motion). A card whose recipe has no photo never issues a fetch at all. One stored resolution serves both the ~200px grid card and the larger detail hero; generating a second "thumbnail" size would mean a second sheet column for a cosmetic gain that isn't worth the contract complexity. Dependency: this assumes the Recipes sheet carries a cheap hasPhoto signal so the list can decide skeleton-vs-placeholder without touching RecipePhotos at all — flagged for the coordinator's contract change, not decided here.
The tag clip's actual cause. recipes.module.css's .tagRow carries flex-wrap:nowrap;overflow:hidden — correct on .cardMeta directly above it (times must stay one line), copied onto the tag pills by mistake. This mock's .rtags has always used flex-wrap:wrap with no overflow at all; that's the one-line diff that fixes it. Tag rows must wrap or truncate a whole word, never clip mid-word — never overflow:hidden without either text-overflow:ellipsis on a genuinely single-line element, or (as here) just letting the row grow.

The detail view doesn't reserve hero space when there's no photo — the grid always does. Different rule, same reasoning as the grid's placeholder, argued from what each screen actually needs: a card grid has neighbours to line up against, so every card claims the same 2:1 box whether or not a photo fills it. The segmented control fix, made concrete. One class, .seg.wrap (grid-template-columns:repeat(auto-fit,minmax(72px,1fr)) instead of a fixed-row inline-flex), applied to every ≤4-option control — not just this one. It costs nothing when there's room: at any width wide enough for four ~72px+ segments, auto-fit resolves to a single row, pixel-identical to today. It only changes behaviour exactly where the current control breaks: a rail too narrow for four full labels. This isn't the sheet from UI_DESIGN.md §5 — that's still reserved for truly large sets — it's making "inline, no overlay" actually mean "always fits," which the threshold already implied but the component never enforced. Add, replace, remove — three states, always visible, never hover-only. Empty state (phone, tablet) is a dashed-border tap target — the same idiom already used for "+ add" slot chips in Settings and the empty Plan slot, so a household that's never seen this control before still recognises it as "add something here." Once a photo exists (desktop), Replace and Remove sit below the thumbnail as ordinary buttons, not a hover overlay — Plan's reroll/pin icons already established that hover-only controls fail on touch, and an iPad in the tablet tier is touch-first. The Photo field sits in the Identity card, right after Name and before Meal tags: identity is what a recipe is called, what it looks like, and how it's tagged, and a photo belongs with the other two rather than sharing a card with prep-time steppers. Not shown: the crop step between picking a file and this state — that's the encoder's 512px/32KB budget being enforced, and it's a behavioural detail for whoever builds the upload flow, not a layout one.

Why four columns, not an auto-fill scroll strip or a horizontal swipe. Three options were on the table. A scrollable strip (phone's day-card pattern, just wider) hides most of the week behind a swipe — exactly the "whole week visible" property that UI_DESIGN.md §13 says justifies desktop in the first place, lost one tier early. Auto-fill/minmax (the same mechanism fixing Recipes and Ingredients) sounds consistent, but on a 7-item grid it produces an orphan: 5 or 6 columns fit, leaving one lonely card alone on its own row. A week reads as a calendar; a calendar with a stray Sunday off by itself reads as broken, not responsive. Four columns, banded avoids both: the whole week is still visible (two short scans instead of one), no orphaned cell, and "weekdays" / "weekend" is a grouping that's already meaningful in a meal planner, not an arbitrary split invented to make the math work.
